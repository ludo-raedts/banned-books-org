# GDR (East Germany) censorship cases 1953–1989 — sources

Compiled 2026-11-12 for `scripts/import-ddr-censorship.ts` (seed: `data/ddr-censorship-2026-11-12.json`).

There is no title-level list of books banned in the GDR after the 1946–1953 removal lists (those are the `sbz-1946` register). This batch is hand-curated. Rules:

- A row needs a censorship act by a state or party body against a **named book**: print permit (Druckgenehmigung) refused, edition pulped or withdrawn, delivery stopped, import blocked, the author punished for the Western edition, or — as "restricted" — forced cuts, a capped print run, or a delay of years.
- Every source below carries the **verbatim sentence** that states the fact. All quotes were re-checked against the cited page on 2026-11-12 (automated substring check on the fetched page; three two-column PDFs/footnoted pages checked by hand).
- Rows resting on Wikipedia alone were left out. Wikipedia appears only as an additional source.
- Biographies on bundesstiftung-aufarbeitung.de ("Wer war wer in der DDR") were consulted during research but are **not cited**: the site blocks automated access, so those quotes could not be re-checked.
- Main source for the 1950s–60s: Simone Barck, Martina Langermann, Siegfried Lokatis, *„Jedes Buch ein Abenteuer“. Zensur-System und literarische Öffentlichkeiten in der DDR bis Ende der sechziger Jahre* (Akademie Verlag, Berlin 1997), open-access reprint ZZF Potsdam, <https://doi.org/10.14765/zzf.dok.1.1045>. It is based on the censorship office's files (Bundesarchiv DR 1). Page numbers are the printed pages.
- `first_published_year` is filled only where a source gives it. Titles of foreign works are given in the original language; the German title of the refused edition is in the description or the quote.

### George Orwell — 1984 (1959, banned)

The novel was never licensed in the GDR and stood on the lists of literature to be removed; possession could lead to prison. In 1959 the young Baldur Haase was arrested by the Stasi in Gera for owning a copy and discussing it in letters, and was held until 1961 (Stasi remand prison Gera, then Waldheim penitentiary) for 'staatsgefährdende Hetze'.
